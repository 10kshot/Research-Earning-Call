Counterparty Contradictions
RESEARCH PLAN · VERSION 2 EARNINGS CALL DISCLOSURE UPDATED SEPT 13, 2026

Your customers tell a different story than your suppliers

Counterparty contradictions and abnormal disclosure in earnings calls: measuring what managers say against benchmarks they can't control.

Objective Design principles Core signal Hypotheses Data Step by step Threats Pilot Changes from v1
Objective

This study builds a measure of disclosure quality in earnings calls that managers cannot easily game, because its benchmark sits outside their own words. Abnormal disclosure (AD) is the part of what managers claim that departs from what their fundamentals and public information imply. Counterparty contradictions (CC) are conflicts between a firm's claims and its suppliers' or customers' claims about the same relationship. Using LLM extraction of structured claims from transcripts linked through the supply-chain network, and fixed, pre-specified rules for matching and resolution, I (1) extract claims and estimate expected disclosure, (2) score contradictions between linked firms, (3) resolve each claim against accounting and market outcomes, and (4) test whether AD predicts contradictions, what predicts being wrong, whether prices absorb contradictions at once or drift where few analysts watch both sides, and whether AD and CC predict volatility, trading activity and bad outcomes beyond tone and risk words.

Working title: "Your Customers Tell a Different Story: Counterparty Contradictions and Abnormal Disclosure in Earnings Calls."

Motivation

Dictionary-based tone and risk measures are losing power because managers now write for machine readers. They avoid flagged words, emphasize minor risks to crowd out major ones, and rehearse their Q&A answers. Any measure built only on a manager's own words can be gamed by that manager.

This design uses two outside benchmarks. AD judges each claim against what the firm's circumstances imply, the way the Jones model judges accruals against what sales and assets imply. CC judges it against what a supplier or customer says about the same relationship, which the focal manager can't script. Both are then checked against what actually happened.

Design principles

The pipeline is built for one researcher. Every choice favors reproducibility and auditability over sophistication.

  • The benchmark is never the manager's own words. Claims are resolved with accounting and market data. Later manager statements are a last-resort fallback and can never clear a claim.
  • Fixed rules over LLM judgment. The LLM has one job: extracting literal claims, checked against a hand-labeled gold set. Date conversion, counterparty matching, contradiction scoring and resolution are done in code.
  • Settle ambiguity at the earliest point. Horizon, counterparty and comparison basis are captured at extraction, then normalized in code right away, not in later steps.
  • Write it down before looking at returns. The claim gate, dimensions, mapping table, resolution rules, check dates, moderators and main specifications are all fixed in advance.
  • Every number traces back to a quote. Each claim carries its transcript, turn, verbatim span, model and prompt version.
The core signal

One relationship, two calls, opposite claims

MEASURE 1 · every firm

Abnormal disclosure (AD)

ADi,t,d = Di,t,d − E[ Di,t,d | Ii,t ]

For firm i, quarter t and dimension d, the gap between what was claimed and what the public information set implies. Signed: positive means rosier than the circumstances warrant.

MEASURE 2 · linked firms

Counterparty contradiction (CC)

CCi,t = Σj wij · Levelij,t

Level = 1 when mapped claims about the same target quarter move against the sign the mapping table expects. Weighted by revenue share w. Timing contradictions, silence and evasion are tracked as separate variables.

How they fit together: CC is the sharp external test but exists only where supply-chain links are observed. AD covers every firm but depends on a model. If AD built without counterparty information predicts CC, AD is validated and can be used for firms with no visible counterparties.

Questions & hypotheses
RQ1

Measurement. Can abnormal disclosure be measured reliably, and does it predict counterparty contradictions?

H1 AD estimated without counterparty data is positively associated with CC in the same quarter.

RQ2

Who is wrong. When linked firms contradict each other, which claims do later outcomes refute?

H2 Claims later resolved as wrong (Tier A evidence) come from firms with higher positive AD, stronger incentives to spin (missed consensus, upcoming equity issuance, insider selling) and a weaker credibility record.

RQ3

Pricing. Do prices absorb contradictions when they become observable, or drift?

H3a Descriptive, uses the later outcome on purpose. The stock of the firm eventually resolved as wrong drifts toward the outcome after the later call instead of adjusting at once.

H3b Tradable, uses only information available at the later call. Firms with high predicted wrongness earn negative abnormal returns. The effect is stronger when fewer of the firm's analysts also cover the counterparty, and when no analyst on the later call raised the counterparty.

RQ4

Activity & risk. Do AD and CC predict volatility, trading activity and bad outcomes beyond tone and risk words?

H4 AD and CC predict post-call realized volatility, abnormal volume, next-quarter earnings misses, guidance cuts and crash risk, controlling for dictionary tone and risk-word counts.

Method

Step by step

  1. Build the data Rules, network, transcripts

  2. Write the rule book before touching the data

    One document, committed to git with a date, that fixes every decision later steps depend on:

    • The claim gate, dimension list, direction convention and hedge levels (step 04)
    • The mapping table with expected sign and lag (step 09)
    • The resolution rules, evidence order and check dates (step 11)
    • Moderator definitions and main regression specifications (steps 13–14)

    Extraction prompts may be revised on the development set. Specifications may not change once return data has been examined; any later change is logged with its reason and reported.

    Output: rulebook_v1, timestamped.

  3. Build the supply-chain network

    • Match Compustat customer-segment names to firm identifiers with fuzzy matching plus manual review. Budget real time for this.
    • Add counterparties named in transcripts and 10-Ks ("our foundry partner").
    • Keep two tiers of links: named (firm to firm) and end-market (firm to "hyperscaler demand", "smartphone OEMs").
    • Pull each firm's fiscal year-end history from Compustat. Fiscal quarters differ across the chain, so calendar conversion depends on it.

    Output: directed supplier → customer edges by quarter, with revenue share wij where known, plus a fiscal calendar table.

  4. Preprocess transcripts

    • Split each call into prepared remarks and Q&A; tag speaker roles (CEO, CFO, other executive, analyst, operator).
    • Split long prepared-remarks turns at paragraph breaks so each unit stays short.
    • Pair every analyst question with its answer, and flag question_mentions_counterparty when the question names or refers to a linked firm.

    Output: turn-level records with transcript ID, call date, section, speaker, turn index, linked question, and fiscal year-end.

  5. Extract claims One LLM job, validated

  6. Write the codebook and build the gold set

    Claim gate. A statement is a claim only if it answers all three: about what (a linked dimension), which way (a direction), when (a horizon). It must also be something later data could show to be wrong. Everything else is a rejected candidate with a reason code: no_direction, no_horizon, reported_fact, boilerplate.

    Dimensions (fixed)

    DimensionCovers
    end_demandDemand for the firm's or customer's end products and markets
    orders_backlogOrders, bookings, backlog, book-to-bill
    inventory_ownThe firm's own inventory
    inventory_downstreamChannel, distributor or customer inventory; digestion; double-ordering
    selling_priceThe firm's own prices and ASPs
    input_costPrices the firm pays for components and materials
    capacity_supplyCapacity, utilization, lead times, allocation, shortages
    capex_procurementCapital spending and purchasing plans
    marginGross or operating margin (used for AD only)
    • Direction is a 5-point ordinal (−2 to +2) for the change in the variable over the target period, not its level. "Inventory is elevated" is not a direction; "we're working inventory down" is −1.
    • Hedge is ordinal: 0 plain assertion, 1 qualified ("we expect"), 2 heavily hedged ("could see some softness"). Hedged claims are kept, never dropped.
    • Extract literally. "Adding a second shift" is a capacity claim; any demand implication is left to the mapping step.
    • Always keep denials ("we're not seeing double-ordering") and conditional claims, with the condition recorded.

    Gold set. Sample turns, oversampling those that touch linked dimensions. Hand-code about 100 turns as a development set and a similar-sized held-out test set. For the paper, a second coder labels 300–500 claims and agreement is reported as Cohen's kappa.

    Output: codebook, development set, held-out set.

  7. Extract claims

    • One structured-output LLM call per turn. Q&A answers are sent with the analyst's question. The call date and fiscal year-end are included as context.
    • Temperature 0, pinned model ID, versioned prompt, with 4–5 examples covering the hard cases: denial, heavy hedge, fiscal-quarter reference, evasion.
    • The LLM does not convert dates, resolve counterparties or report its own confidence.
    {
  "transcript_id": "S_FY2024Q3_call",
  "call_date": "2024-10-24",
  "turn_index": 47,
  "segment": "qa",
  "speaker_role": "CEO",
  "question_turn_index": 46,
  "source_text": "[verbatim turn]",
  "run": { "model": "…", "prompt_version": "extract_v1", "temperature": 0 },

  "claims": [{
    "span": "[exact supporting quote]",
    "subject_scope": "anonymous_counterparty",   // own | named_counterparty | anonymous_counterparty | end_market | industry
    "counterparty_ref": "our largest compute customer",
    "dimension": "orders_backlog",
    "direction": 2,                               // −2 … +2, change over target period
    "horizon_text": "into Q4",
    "horizon_ref": "fiscal_quarter",              // fiscal_quarter | calendar | relative | unspecified
    "comparison_basis": "sequential",             // sequential | year_over_year | unspecified
    "hedge": 0,                                   // 0 | 1 | 2
    "quantified": false, "quant_value": null, "quant_unit": null,
    "is_denial": false,
    "condition": null
  }],

  "rejected_candidates": [
    { "span": "…", "dimension": "end_demand", "reason": "no_direction" }
  ]
}

    Output: claim table and rejected-candidate table, one row per claim, each traceable to its quote.

  8. Validate before going further

    • Tune the prompt on the development set only. Score once on the held-out set.
    • Pass bar on the held-out set: dimension accuracy ≥ 85%, direction accuracy ≥ 80%, and opposite-sign errors (up read as down) reported separately and kept rare (for example ≤ 5%). These drive false contradictions.
    • Report recall, not just precision. The silence measure in step 10 is only as good as recall.
    • Below the bar: fix the prompt. Don't move on to matching.
    • In production, rerun a random sample of turns and report how often the runs agree.

    Output: validation report by field and dimension.

  9. Normalize in code

    • Convert horizon_text + horizon_ref to calendar target_start and target_end using the call date and fiscal calendar. "Second half" becomes a two-quarter range, not one quarter.
    • Resolve counterparty_ref to a firm ID using the network and revenue-concentration disclosures. Unresolved references stay at the end-market tier.
    • Remove duplicate claims within a call, keeping repeat_count and qa_softer_than_script (the Q&A version is weaker than the prepared remarks).

    Output: normalized claim table, the single source of truth for everything after.

  10. Measure AD, contradictions, silence

  11. Estimate expected disclosure and compute AD

    • (a) Regression baseline. For each dimension, regress firm-quarter disclosure (net direction, emphasis share, hedging rate, quantification rate) on the information set: earnings surprise, sales growth, inventory change, margin change, guidance change, quarter return, peers' same-quarter disclosure and firm fixed effects. Estimate by industry-quarter on past data only. The residual is AD.
    • (b) LLM counterfactual. Give an LLM an anonymized information packet and have it produce the claims an unbiased manager would make, in the same schema. Compare with actual claims.
    • Two versions: ADno-cp without counterparty information (for H1 and H3b) and ADfull with it (for H4).

    Output: signed AD by firm, quarter and dimension.

  12. Match claims and score contradictions

    Build aggregated views from the claim table at the (firm, subject, dimension, target quarter) level, keeping hedge and section. Then compare linked cells with a fixed mapping table, not an LLM judge:

    Supplier dimensionCustomer dimensionExpectedAllowed lag
    inventory_downstreaminventory_ownsame sign0 (same inventory)
    selling_priceinput_costsame sign0
    orders_backlogcapex_procurementsame sign0
    orders_backlogend_demandsame sign0–1 quarter
    capacity_supplycapacity_supplysame sign0
    • Level contradiction: directions disagree with the expected sign for overlapping target quarters.
    • Timing contradiction: directions agree but the move falls outside the allowed lag. Often honest bullwhip effects, so kept separate.
    • Compare claims with the same subject only. A supplier's claim about total demand is not matched against one customer's purchasing.
    • Heavily hedged claims (hedge = 2) are excluded from level-contradiction scoring but count for AD.

    Output: CC by link-quarter and firm-quarter, dated at the later call.

  13. Detect silence and evasion

    • Silence: the counterparty has a claim in a linked (dimension, quarter) cell and the focal firm has none. A fixed rule run after extraction, reported with the extractor's recall.
    • Evasion: an analyst asked about a linked dimension and the answer produced no claim (only rejected candidates, or nothing). This is kept separate from silence and is likely the stronger signal.

    Output: silence and evasion counts by firm, dimension and quarter.

  14. Resolve Score each claim against outcomes

  15. Resolve each claim with an evidence hierarchy

    Each side's claim is scored against its own outcome; no winner is picked per pair. Evidence is used in a fixed order by dimension. Manager statements can never clear a claim.

    DimensionTier A: hard dataTier B: indirect proxy
    orders_backlogBacklog or remaining performance obligations; the counterparty's implied purchases; actuals vs. guidance given at the callSupplier total or segment revenue
    capex_procurementCapex (year-to-date, differenced); purchase obligations; implied purchasesSupplier revenue
    inventory_ownBalance-sheet inventory, days inventory—
    inventory_downstreamThe counterparty's inventory; distributor inventoriesIndustry billings data
    end_demandRevenue vs. guidance given at the call; monthly revenue where reportedIndustry billings and shipment data
    selling_price, input_costProducer price index; memory spot and contract pricesGross margin change
    capacity_supplyCapexDisclosed utilization; lead-time surveys
    marginGross margin from reported sales and COGS—
     
    Implied purchases_t = COGS_t + Inventory_t − Inventory_(t−1)

    Decision rules

    • Direction: realized change is measured on the claim's comparison basis (default: sequential), minus the firm's average change in the same fiscal quarter over the prior 3–5 years, then binned into the same −2 to +2 scale using the firm's own history.
    • No-call zone: a realized change within one standard deviation of the firm's normal seasonal change counts as flat.
    • Best tier wins: use the highest tier available. If that tier has two or more signals and they disagree, the label is conflicted.
    • Check date, fixed: when first-reported numbers for target_end are out, plus one more quarter so late moves can be labeled.

    Labels per claim

    right
    Realized direction has the claimed sign
    wrong
    Realized direction has the opposite sign (the H2 outcome)
    flat
    Realized change inside the no-call zone
    early / late
    Move matched the claim one quarter off the target
    conflicted
    Signals within the best tier disagree
    unresolved
    No Tier A or B evidence
    • Tiers: A = hard data on the dimension and horizon (main H2 test); B = indirect proxy; C = only later manager statements, retrieved and quoted by an LLM, reviewed by a person, robustness only.
    • Pair outcomes: when both sides of a level contradiction are resolved as right, it was a false contradiction. That rate is reported as a validity check on CC.
    • Report how resolved claims differ from unresolved ones (size, quantification, dimension).

    Output: a resolution table with label, tier and evidence used for every claim.

  16. Build the credibility record

    For each firm on each call date: the share of its past claims labeled wrong, counting only claims whose check date came before that call. Shrink toward the sample average when a firm has few resolved claims.

    Output: a real-time credibility score, usable in H2 and H3b without look-ahead.

  17. Test Moderators, hypotheses, robustness

  18. Build the H3 moderators

    RoleVariableDefinition
    MainAnalyst overlapShare of firm i's analysts who also cover counterparty j, from I/B/E/S detail estimates in the 12 months before the later call. Measured from i's side, because H3 is about i's price.
    MainCross-referenceAn analyst on the later call asked about the counterparty or its statements (question_mentions_counterparty).
    ControlCall gapDays between the two calls, and which firm reported second. Mostly driven by fixed fiscal calendars, so used with pair fixed effects.
    RobustnessAlternativesJaccard overlap; 90-day window; shared broker; any-overlap indicator.

    All moderators are built and reported, whatever they show. Controls: each firm's analyst count, revenue share, size, institutional ownership, same-industry indicator, link type.

    Output: moderator panel by link-quarter.

  19. Test the hypotheses

    H1   CC_i,t          = a + b·AD_nocp_i,t + controls + FE(firm, quarter)

H2   Wrong_claim     = a + b1·AD_nocp + b2·Incentive + b3·Credibility + FE(pair)
     (LPM / logit, Tier A; full sample as robustness)

H3a  CAR of eventually-wrong firm over [t+2, t+60] vs. matched controls
     (descriptive: how fast prices reach the outcome)

H3b  CAR_i,[t+2,t+60] = a + b1·PredWrong_i,t
                        + b2·PredWrong × Overlap + b3·PredWrong × CrossRef
                        + gap controls + controls + FE(industry × quarter)
     PredWrong: H2 model re-estimated on an expanding window, past data only
     also: counterparty's abnormal return on the later call day;
           calendar-time portfolios, FF5 + momentum alphas

H4   Y_i,t+k         = a + b1·AD_full_i,t + b2·CC_i,t + tone + LM risk words + controls
     Y ∈ { realized vol [t+1,t+5], abnormal volume, ΔIV, SUE_t+1,
           guidance cut, NCSKEW_t+1 }

    Controls: size, book-to-market, momentum, earnings surprise, dictionary tone, risk-word counts, and customer return momentum (following Cohen & Frazzini) so CC isn't just picking up known return lags along supply chains. Use continuous moderators in regressions; high/low splits for plots only.

  20. Robustness

    • Placebo: random same-industry pairs with no economic link. CC should not predict outcomes.
    • Look-ahead bias in the LLM: strip names and dates, use a model whose training data ends before the test period, and test whether it can guess the firm from the packet.
    • Measurement: rerun on hand-coded claims only, and with alternative prompts and models.
    • Scope: named links only; level contradictions only; Tier A only vs. all tiers.
    • Identification (full sample, if events exist): broker closures and mergers that remove a shared analyst.

Decision gate before scaling

  • Extraction passes the held-out bar (dimension ≥ 85%, direction ≥ 80%, opposite-sign errors rare).
  • Enough level contradictions to test, for example 20 or more across the pilot chain.
  • Most flagged contradictions are real: false-contradiction rate well below half.
  • A meaningful share of claims resolvable at Tier A.

If contradictions are rare, shift the paper's weight to AD and use CC as validation. The pilot is too small for the H3 moderator tests; treat those results as descriptive.

Contribution
  • An external benchmark for disclosure quality that the disclosing manager can't control.
  • The logic of abnormal accruals applied to narrative disclosure, at the level of individual claims rather than document tone.
  • A credibility record for managers, built from resolved claims and available in real time.
  • Evidence on limited attention along economic links: do investors read both sides of a relationship?
Changes from v1
  • Contradictions are scored with a fixed mapping table (expected sign and lag) instead of an LLM judge.
  • Claims pass an explicit gate; the schema adds counterparty scope, comparison basis, horizon ranges, ordinal hedging, denials and rejected candidates.
  • Fiscal-to-calendar conversion and counterparty matching moved from the LLM into code.
  • Resolution uses an evidence hierarchy by dimension with fixed check dates and first-reported data. The LLM no longer drafts labels, and manager statements can't clear a claim.
  • H3 is split into H3a and H3b to remove look-ahead; a credibility record is added.
  • Analyst overlap is measured from the focal firm's side; a transcript cross-reference moderator is added; all moderators are fixed in advance.
  • A rule book is written before any data work.
